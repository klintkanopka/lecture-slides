# Introduction

<v-clicks depth="2">

- It's basically impossible to do anything without hearing about how AI and large language models are changing the future of science, work, and research
  - This has led to a pile of research in the psychological and behavioral sciences of "varying quality"
- A somewhat vocal subset of social scientists have become quite excited about _silicon sampling_
  - This is the idea that large language models can potentially replace human subjects; sounds efficient!
  - See: Argyle, L. P., Busby, E. C., Fulda, N., Gubler, J. R., Rytting, C., & Wingate, D. (2023). [Out of one, many: Using language models to simulate human samples](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/political-analysis/article/out-of-one-many-using-language-models-to-simulate-human-samples/035D7C8A55B237942FB6DBAD7CAA4E49). _Political Analysis_, 31(3), 337-351.
- Here's what I think:<img
  style="position: absolute; top: 30px; left: 600px; width: 350px"
  src="/cloud.jpg"
  alt=""
/>
  - If we are concerned with measuring latent constructs in humans and constructing instruments for that task, humans should be involved
  - If LLMs are useful for this task, we should treat them as _data augmentation_, not _data generation_
  - LLMs aren't guaranteed to produce the same ability distributions as humans, so we should incorporate their responses from the perspective of _scale linking with diminished confidence_

</v-clicks>
